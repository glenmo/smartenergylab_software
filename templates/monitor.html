How the controlled loads are operated

All the loads run off the Wombat Hollow Microgrid, so the aim is to use solar the batteries can't store without ever running a load from the batteries or the generator. Two controllers do the switching, in priority order: hot water first, then space heating at the Lodge. When the batteries are full the SP Pro raises the microgrid frequency above 50 Hz to curtail solar, so a frequency above 50.2 Hz means solar is being wasted.

1. Hot water: Wharenui, then Lodge

Both tanks have my-PV AC‑THOR 9s diverters (up to 9 kW each), driven over Modbus every 5 s by acthor-surplus on rubberduck.

  • Heating starts when the SP Pro is above 95 % and charging at more than 5 kW (only the charge above 5 kW is diverted), or when the frequency is above 50.2 Hz. In that case heater power rises 1 kW per 5 s, holds down to 50.1 Hz, and falls below that.
  • The Wharenui gets the surplus first. The Lodge gets what's left, and so does anything a hot tank doesn't draw.
  • Power ramps up slowly and drops at once. It's cut straight away if the batteries discharge more than 300 W.
  • No heating while the generator runs, nor for 5 minutes after. No heating either when the SP Pro data is more than 60 s old, or when a diverter is unreachable or in its blocking window.
  • Cloudy-day boost (Wharenui only): from 15:00 to 18:00, if the tank is below 60 °C and surplus gives less than 3 kW, one element heats at about 3 kW until 63 °C. It never runs while the generator does.

2. Lodge heating: heater plugs and aircons

Run by Home Assistant at the Lodge (LodgyBox) from the same microgrid data.

  • Surplus means both batteries above 95 %, or the frequency above 50.2 Hz. On top of that, the SP Pro must be at 70 % or more and the generator off, for 5 minutes in a row.
  • Nothing starts until both hot water tanks are satisfied (hot, at full power, or not taking surplus).
  • Units are then added one at a time, at least 5 minutes apart, while the SP Pro is still charging above 5 kW or the frequency is above 50.2 Hz. The order is Upstairs heater, Office heater, Lounge aircon (heat), Dining Room aircon (heat).
  • When the surplus ends, the data goes stale, or the batteries discharge more than 300 W for 2 minutes, units go off in reverse order, 5 minutes apart.
  • Surplus heating has a master switch and a switch for each room in Home Assistant, plus a heating setpoint for the aircons (the heaters use their own thermostats). Turning a switch off stops its units at once.
  • The automation only turns off units it turned on. If someone changes one of those units by hand, the automation stops managing it.
  • The Caretaker's Flat aircon is never controlled by surplus and isn't shown here.

3. Smart Energy Lab aircons: Dacha and Studio

These are monitored only and are switched by hand (Daikin app or the hub dashboard). They show Heating or Cooling while the compressor is actually running and Idle while the unit is on but resting at its setpoint. Their power figure is estimated from the unit's 0.1 kWh energy counter, so it can lag by a minute or two.

Reading this page

  • A surplus tag means the unit is currently switched by one of the controllers above.
  • A stale tag means the unit's data hasn't updated recently. Its last known state is still shown.
  • Microgrid demand is the power both inverters put onto the microgrid (plus any house solar export), worked out the same way as the power-flow diagram. A battery that's charging draws from the microgrid.
  • The generator counts as running when the SP Pro's AC input is 200 W or more.
